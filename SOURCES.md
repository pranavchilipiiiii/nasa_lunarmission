# Data, attribution, and AI assistance

## NASA/JPL data
- [Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/), [API documentation](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html), [manual](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html).
- `data/horizons-year-raw.txt` preserves the full returned header, table, units, and explanatory footer.
- `data/ephemeris-year.json` contains the exact query URL, retrieval timestamp, column meanings, and 8,761 hourly samples, 2026-11-14 through 2027-11-14 UTC.
- Run `python3 scripts/fetch_data.py` to reproduce. The query uses the Moon as target, Earth center as observer, quantities 14/15 (apparent sub-observer/sub-solar coordinates) and 20 (range, km).

## Model
Smooth reference sphere, radius 1737.4 km. Elevation uses the dot product of a surface normal and the subpoint direction. Earth elevation includes finite-distance surface parallax using each row's actual Earth distance. Directions and distances retain Horizons' apparent/light-time conventions. The simple geometry is a prototype approximation, not an exact terrain or navigation model. Interpolation between hourly samples uses shortest-path longitude interpolation.

See [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md): 156 reproducible elevation comparisons across two rounds, plus 18 independently retrieved off-hour comparisons at two 2027 dates. An additional 720 elevation and sky-projected azimuth comparisons cover all 15 catalog sites at 24 times across the year; worst sampled differences are Sun 0.000776°, Earth 0.005444° and sky-projected azimuth 0.005460°. Raw responses and query URLs are in `data/validation-rounds-raw.json` and `data/validation-catalog-raw.json`; run `node tests/validation-rounds.test.mjs` and `node tests/validation-catalog.test.mjs`. Azimuth is now implemented, with checks on 108 of these body/site samples. Combined worst sampled differences are Sun elevation 0.0009°, Earth elevation 0.0059°, and sky-projected azimuth 0.0059°. These are sample results, not a global error bound. Extended-date raw NASA responses and exact query URLs are committed. Analytic disc-area fractions are not covered by these comparisons. Sun angular radius is fixed at 0.267°; Earth angular radius is based on finite observer distance.

No measured topography in the calculations, real terrain occultation, local slopes, eclipses, radio-link budget, relay coverage, full power-system model, or equipment thermal analysis. User-defined simplified scenarios are documented below. Elevations and crossing times refer to body centers. Partial-disc area above the smooth horizon is estimated separately; no first/last disc contact times are calculated. No crossing in the cached period does not mean a body never rises/sets. Event dates are interpolated estimates.

## Team-requested lunar cycle and planning tools
- Lunar phase timing: [US Naval Observatory primary phases API](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/api.html), cached for 2026 and 2027 in `data/lunar-phases.json`, including the complete original responses and exact query URLs. The service publishes Universal Time; the interface aligns it to the UTC timeline at minute precision, without a sub-second UT1/UTC conversion. The age and period use consecutive official new-Moon times. Primary phase labels apply within six hours of the published event; between them the labels describe waxing/waning crescent/gibbous stages. The phase preview is a NASA-map-textured sphere viewed along the cached sub-Earth direction, lunar north up, and lit along the sub-solar direction. Its terminator follows the same geometry as the illuminated fraction; brightness and faint unlit-side fill are illustrative rather than calibrated photometry. It is not oriented to a particular ground observer on Earth.
- Approximate illuminated fraction of the Earth-facing lunar disc: `(1 + dot(earthDirection, sunDirection))/2` using the cached Horizons subpoint directions. Seven direct NASA quantity-10 comparisons from Nov 14–Dec 14, 2026 differ by at most 0.0000041 percentage point. Exact query, raw response and values: `tests/lunar-phase-reference.json`. Re-fetch with `python3 scripts/validate_phase.py`; re-test with `node tests/mission.test.mjs`. Eclipse dimming is excluded. [NASA's phase explanation](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/) distinguishes the Earth-view phase from local lunar illumination.
- Mission catalog: 15 lander coordinates in `data/landing-sites.json`, sourced from the [NASA NSSDCA spacecraft table](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/lunar_artifact_impacts.html). Six Apollo, six international and three CLPS locations; DE421 mean Earth/polar-axis frame, east positive. Values are source coordinates for exploration, not surveyed safe landing zones. The map is a general visual model and does not establish precision landmark positioning.
- Visibility intervals integrate the piecewise-linear hourly **center elevation above 0°**, clipped at the displayed chart or landing-relative 30-day boundary. Intervals reaching a boundary are labeled as clipped. No post-cache time is extrapolated. Event countdowns refer to simulation time, not the user's wall clock. First/last disc contact and terrain occultation are excluded.
- The surface sky is a panoramic bearing/elevation diagram. Markers are centers and are not drawn at angular scale; points below −30° are clamped with a downward cue and their actual elevation is given in the caption.

## Simplified estimates — assumptions, not mission predictions
The user explicitly approved labeled simplified estimates. A uniform, user-entered obstruction angle is a hypothetical skyline, **not extracted from the 3D mesh or a measured DEM**. It affects the estimates tab and the dashed skyline in the surface-sky tab; base site elevations, operational windows and comparisons remain the smooth 0°-horizon results.

Solar irradiance is fixed at 1361 W/m² ([NASA solar irradiance](https://earth.gsfc.nasa.gov/climate/projects/solar-irradiance/science)). Incident flux on a **horizontal** plate is `1361 × max(0, sin(centerElevation)) × visibleDiscAreaFraction`. Electrical output is incident flux × panel area × conversion efficiency. This approximates partial-disc irradiance using center-angle incidence; it omits tracking, distance variation, dust, eclipse, thermal degradation, wiring, batteries and power-system losses.

The thermal scenario is a **separate horizontal reference plate**, not the solar panel or lunar ground: solar absorptivity α=0.9, infrared emissivity ε=0.9, one emitting face, no conduction, thermal inertia, internal heat, reflected light or other heat sources. Absorbed heat is α × incident flux. Solar-only radiative balance uses `T = (absorbedHeat/(εσ))^(1/4)`, σ=5.670374419×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴. In darkness no temperature is displayed; a zero-K result would misrepresent omitted heat sources. Unit checks verify equation balance and limiting cases; no equipment/terrain model validation is claimed.
