      Assumptions and formulas

      Solar flux is fixed at 1361 W/m² at normal incidence. A horizontal panel receives flux × max(0, sin(Sun-center elevation)) × unobstructed disc-area fraction. Electrical output multiplies this by area and conversion efficiency. No tracking, dust, temperature loss, wiring loss, storage, eclipse or distance variation is modeled. Thermal values describe a separate horizontal reference plate: absorbed flux = 0.9 × incident flux, and T = (absorbed flux / (0.9 × σ))¼ with one emitting face, σ = 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴. Conduction, internal heat, thermal inertia, reflected light and other heat sources are excluded. No sun-only temperature is shown in darkness. Use real terrain and equipment models for an engineering assessment.

      A LITTLE CONTEXT

      Explore. Compare. Understand.

      Drag the Moon or enter coordinates, choose a UTC date, and pin two locations. Positive elevation means the body’s center is above the smooth modeled horizon. The main chart follows your selected location. The planning tabs below add Earth-view lunar phases, a panoramic surface sky, visibility durations, same-site date comparisons and simplified power/thermal scenarios.

      What this prototype does not tell you

      The detailed 3D relief is for display only; calculations still use a smooth reference sphere. Mountains and crater rims can block views. Earth visibility is not a radio-link budget. The main elevation readings describe geometry. The Simple estimates tab separately models a hypothetical obstruction, horizontal solar panel and radiative reference plate with visible assumptions; it is not an equipment forecast. Near-horizon results are sensitive to model approximations. This is an exploration tool, not flight-planning software.

      Data & method

      Cached NASA/JPL Horizons sub-observer and sub-solar coordinates, hourly from November 14, 2026 through November 14, 2027. Local spherical geometry estimates elevations; Earth distance from the cache corrects surface parallax. Linear interpolation is used between hourly samples. The apparent Horizons quantities have light-time conventions; see the source documentation and validation notes.

      Validation covers 174 sampled elevation comparisons: 156 reported by Claude plus 18 reproducible checks at two extended-range dates. Worst sampled differences: Sun 0.0009°, Earth 0.0059°. Azimuth was checked on 108 off-hour comparisons, with worst sky-projected difference 0.0059°. These are sampled checks, not a global error bound. See the validation report for scope, missing requests and extended-date tests.

      Direction is clockwise from lunar north; it is undefined at the exact poles or overhead. Disc visibility estimates visible area above a smooth horizon, without terrain, eclipses or refraction. Sun radius is fixed at 0.267°; Earth's radius uses cached distance. Crossing times refer to the body's center, not first or last disc contact. HD is an optional 18.4 MB download with an 8K texture; standard detail is the default on every device.
