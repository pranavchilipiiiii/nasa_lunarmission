How to use

  1. Drag the Moon to rotate it; scroll or pinch to zoom.
  2. Click the surface to select a location. Its latitude and longitude appear in the card.
  3. Choose a UTC date and time, drag the 30-day slider, or click the chart. The Moon’s lighting follows the Sun direction for that moment.
  4. Read Sun and Earth elevation. Above 0° means above the horizon.
  5. Use “Pin as Site A” and “Pin as Site B” on two locations to compare them at the same time.

Developer notes: connecting validated data

Call MIDAS.setProvider({label,start,end,directions(tMs)}), where start/end are UTC ms and directions returns {sun:[x,y,z],earth:[x,y,z]} unit vectors in the Moon body-fixed frame (X toward mean Earth, Z north). Or serve data/horizons-cache.json: {source,start,stepMinutes,sun:[[x,y,z]…],earth:[[x,y,z]…]}. An optional elevation(latDeg,lonDeg,tMs)→{sun,earth} overrides the default smooth-sphere geometry (elevation = asin of site normal · direction from the Moon’s center). Frame and query details must be confirmed with the data owner.
