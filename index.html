How to read this

Drag the Moon or enter coordinates, choose a UTC date, and pin two locations. Positive elevation means the body’s center is above the smooth modeled horizon. The main chart follows your selected location.

What this prototype does not tell you

The detailed 3D relief is for display only; calculations still use a smooth reference sphere. Mountains and crater rims can block views. Earth visibility is not a radio-link budget. Sun elevation is not generated watts or a thermal forecast. Near-horizon results are sensitive to model approximations. This is an exploration tool, not flight-planning software.

Data & method

Cached NASA/JPL Horizons sub-observer and sub-solar coordinates, hourly from November 14 through December 14, 2026. Local spherical geometry estimates elevations; Earth distance from the cache corrects surface parallax. Linear interpolation is used between hourly samples. The apparent Horizons quantities have light-time conventions; see the source documentation and validation notes.

Claude reported tests at 3 sites across 11 midnight-UTC dates: Sun differences under 0.001°, Earth under 0.02° using mean-distance correction. Those limited tests do not establish a global error bound. This implementation uses the cached varying Earth distance. Team verification is still required; azimuth is intentionally omitted.
