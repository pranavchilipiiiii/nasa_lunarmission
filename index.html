A LITTLE CONTEXT

Explore. Compare. Understand.

Drag the Moon or enter coordinates, choose a UTC date, and pin two locations. Positive elevation means the body’s center is above the smooth modeled horizon. The main chart follows your selected location.

What this prototype does not tell you

The detailed 3D relief is for display only; calculations still use a smooth reference sphere. Mountains and crater rims can block views. Earth visibility is not a radio-link budget. Sun elevation is not generated watts or a thermal forecast. Near-horizon results are sensitive to model approximations. This is an exploration tool, not flight-planning software.

Data & method

Cached NASA/JPL Horizons sub-observer and sub-solar coordinates, hourly from November 14, 2026 through November 14, 2027. Local spherical geometry estimates elevations; Earth distance from the cache corrects surface parallax. Linear interpolation is used between hourly samples. The apparent Horizons quantities have light-time conventions; see the source documentation and validation notes.

Validation covers 174 sampled elevation comparisons: 156 reported by Claude plus 18 reproducible checks at two extended-range dates. Worst sampled differences: Sun 0.0009°, Earth 0.0059°. Azimuth was checked on 108 off-hour comparisons, with worst sky-projected difference 0.0059°. These are sampled checks, not a global error bound. See the validation report for scope, missing requests and extended-date tests.

Direction is clockwise from lunar north; it is undefined at the exact poles or overhead. Disc visibility estimates visible area above a smooth horizon, without terrain, eclipses or refraction. Sun radius is fixed at 0.267°; Earth's radius uses cached distance. Crossing times refer to the body's center, not first or last disc contact. HD is an optional 18.4 MB download with an 8K texture; standard detail is the default on every device.
